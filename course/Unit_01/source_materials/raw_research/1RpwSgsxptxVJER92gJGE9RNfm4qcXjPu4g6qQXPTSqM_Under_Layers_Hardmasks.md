# Underlayers & Hardmasks: The Stencil and its Foundation

These are other chemical layers used alongside the photoresist to ensure the pattern is transferred perfectly.

* **Underlayers:** These are applied to the wafer *before* the resist. A key type is an anti-reflective coating. It prevents light from bouncing off the wafer and blurring the pattern, which helps keep the circuit lines (**Critical Dimensions**, or CDs) perfectly sharp.  
* **Hardmasks:** The photoresist layer is very thin and fragile. It's not strong enough to withstand the harsh chemical etching process that carves the circuit into the silicon. So, the pattern is first transferred from the delicate resist to a tougher intermediate layer—the **hardmask**. This hardmask then acts as a durable stencil for the final etching step.  
* **How Suppliers Help** They design hardmask materials with high **"etch selectivity."** This means the etching chemicals eat away at the silicon much faster than they eat away at the hardmask. A better hardmask is more resilient, allowing for the creation of taller, thinner circuit features without them bending or falling over (**line collapse**).
